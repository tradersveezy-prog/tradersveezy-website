## In your own words

### What makes a level “matter”?

Not every high/low. Levels where buyers and sellers already showed up — prior reactions, value edges, obvious stops. Random lines = noise.

**Higher timeframes take precedence** — especially when they line up with 1H–4H levels.

### Volume profile — how you use it

- **POC** — where the most trading happened (fair value magnet / acceptance)
- **VAL** — low end of value; reclaim story (ties to your range law)
- **VAH** — high end of value; often where longs get heavy / shorts defend

### Premium / discount

Pullbacks into discount (cheaper relative to the impulse) beat chasing mid-air or premium for longs. Mirror for shorts.

Plain talk: premium / discount are really **VAH / VAL thinking** — where value sits — not mysticism.

### Horizontal / swing levels

Prior swing highs/lows that printed reactions. Critical when held more than once (see NEAR 4.60).

Equal highs / liquidity: teach what it means for **real buyers and sellers** (stops, breaks), not jargon for jargon’s sake.

### Fibs

Used as a map for discount shelves on the impulse leg — not magic numbers alone.

Often gives you a **zone to build off of** — never simply “the” long/short. Fibs only speed up finding potential zones. **Other things in that zone give it strength:** POC, VAL/VAH, FVG, bullish OB, untested broken level, long wicks, etc.

### Mechanical EMAs — 55 & 200

Note the **55 EMA and 200 EMA**. They often provide what traders call a **mechanical bounce** — broker / desk programs are frequently set to execute buy or sell flows at those averages. Watch them as living levels, not just trend filters.

### What you refuse

- Trading the middle of value with no reclaim thesis
- Drawing a wall of lines “just in case” — **if you can’t see the level in 15 seconds, it shouldn’t be used**
- Treating every equal high as a must-trade
- ICT complexity for its own sake — learn how concepts map to people (buyers/sellers), not names
